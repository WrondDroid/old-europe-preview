Since 1948

Two families, one dining room

Old Europe has stood on Wisconsin Avenue for more than seventy-five years. It has had exactly two families at the door.

1948

The Lichtensteins

Ida and Albert Lichtenstein opened Old Europe in 1948 with a simple idea: serve delicious European cooking, pour the best international wines, and keep great German beer on tap.

According to The Georgetowner’s 2024 reporting, the couple had fled Germany and spent time in Ecuador before they reached Washington and opened the restaurant.

Their recipe for Schnitzel Old Europe is still on the menu, made the way they made it: unbreaded veal, chicken ragout fin, tomato and hollandaise, finished in the oven.

1970s to today

The Herolds

Karl Herold worked alongside the Lichtensteins for ten years before taking over the reins in the early 1970s with his wife, Isabel. The restaurant has stayed in the family ever since.

Today the Old Europe team is led by Alex and Cindy Herold. The Georgetowner, visiting in 2024, found Alex as owner and host, describing the food as Bürgerliche Küche, German homestyle cooking.

That means food that is well prepared, generously served and authentically German, brought to the table by staff in traditional dirndls.

The house

Three rooms, a great many steins

The interior is decidedly old world. Antique steins, fine oil paintings and original works of art cover the walls, and for the length of a dinner you are somewhere else entirely. Ask about live music when you call.
